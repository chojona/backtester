## Independent mathematical result

For each active interval, return 1 is `log(first close / first open)` and later returns are `log(close_i / close_(i−1))`. The first return after the halt restarts from that bar's own open. RV is `sum(r²)`. BPV uses `(π/2) sum |r_(i−1)r_i|` only for adjacent returns within the same active interval **and** the same overnight/RTH/post-RTH research segment, matching the frozen boundary convention. Thus neither a return nor a BPV pair spans the five-hour halt. Complete 5-minute blocks are anchored to the active and research-segment openings; each holiday has 228 + 276 complete blocks. The independent test reads Parquet directly and recomputes these values without calling the builder's math.

The 5-minute reference also matches the JSON segment values. RV at 5 minutes need not equal 1-minute RV because five returns are summed before squaring. On the corrected trade-date index, Tuesday appears **once** in a 5- or 22-session window, followed by Wednesday as the next target. The tests check a 22-date feature slice, its last-five slice, Tuesday-close forecast origin, and that every feature interval ends by the origin while the target ends later. Within W1 alone, Jun 21 has fewer than 22 preceding candidate dates, so no 22-session W1-only feature row can originate there. This is an alignment and availability check, not a HAR fit or a claim that provisional dates are eligible training data.

## Recovery assessment

The historical W1 ledger has 120 rows: 92 B and 28 C. Collapsing exactly three Monday/Tuesday pairs removes three duplicate trade-date identities, leaving **117** official candidate dates: 92 historically B-labeled rows and 25 historically C-labeled rows. Labels are retained only for comparison; their meaning has not been updated. The longest currently B-labeled uninterrupted stretch on the corrected index is **21 trade dates** (Sep 9–Oct 7). If only candidate timestamp coverage were considered, the longest full-grid stretch is **38** (Aug 19–Oct 12), but calendar and causal gates still apply. Even if every W1 date were later proved eligible, the maximum W1 run is **117** (Jun 7–Nov 21). W1 therefore cannot contain 120 consecutive eligible CME trade dates.

The smallest numerical contiguous extension is **three additional official trade dates**, subject to confirming their calendars and coverage. Two three-date edge choices are:

|---|---|---|
| Earlier | Jun 2, 3, 6 | Jun 6 has one unexplained selected active-minute hole at Jun 5 23:27 UTC; Jun 2 depends on Jun 1's provisional holiday/causal evidence. The ordinary date schedules and prior-volume chain still need review. |

A dated Thanksgiving NQ schedule must establish its trade-date assignment and expected active minutes, followed by independent evidence for any remaining active holes and corrected causal predecessors. |

The later preview in the JSON is provisional and does **not** assert that Nov 24 is an independently valid official trade date under the Thanksgiving exception. The earlier three-date extension is the cleaner date-count route if June 6's hole and the Jun 1→2 dependency can be adjudicated. Either route still inherits W1's four isolated holes, unresolved ordinary-day calendar review, nine guarded dates not established by the three recovered holiday files (the three following Wednesdays and Oct/Nov guard groups), and their causal-roll propagation. Source-volume completeness for thin contracts also remains unproved. No paid Databento request was made.
